# AHS Art Direction

This is the visual brief for the prototype and the handover guide for a real shoot. It distinguishes three kinds of imagery so that a campaign image is never made to do catalogue work.

## The AHS Visual Language

- **Campaign:** warm, cinematic Indian portraiture. Jewellery leads the frame, with deep emerald appearing only in bridal and assurance moments. Preserve room for copy and keep faces natural, never overly retouched.
- **Catalogue:** an ivory studio field, centred product, restrained shadow, 4:5 crop. Every product should be shown at the same apparent scale within its category. The interface supplies the ivory stage and gentle grade; source photographs still need consistent lighting.
- **Proof:** close, practical views of the clasp, hallmark, workmanship and piece worn for scale. These should feel factual, not campaign-like.

## Required Photography Per Piece

1. Front catalogue frame on a pale, clean background.
2. Side or reverse view.
3. Macro of setting, clasp or texture.
4. Hallmark/HUID detail where the piece is hallmarked.
5. Worn view at true scale, where appropriate.

Name files by product code and view: `AHS-BR-101-front`, `AHS-BR-101-clasp`, `AHS-BR-101-worn`.

## Interface Rules

- Cards use the `ProductValueLockup`: purity, gross weight, then approximate value. Do not rearrange the order.
- The `AhsAssurance` seal means only three things: weighed in front of the customer, BIS hallmark/HUID, and price explained at that day’s rate. It is not an award, an external certification or a price guarantee.
- Emerald is reserved for assurance, hallmark and occasional bridal detail. It should not become a general button or navigation colour.
- The monogram appears as a maker’s seal, a loader, app icon and quiet assurance mark. The wordmark remains the primary brand signature.
- Jaali and kundan-petal motifs frame information or signify craftsmanship. Never use them as all-over wallpaper.
